Research Notes

Reading, Critiquing, and Extending Research in Human-AI Interaction

These notes are designed as research-building exercises rather than summaries. Each note will identify a paper’s question, method, contribution, limits, and a possible extension into my own research agenda.

Placeholder Note

AI Trust & Reliance

Possible extension question: “Can repeated interaction with an AI system increase perceived trust even when its actual reliability remains unchanged?”

Paper Overview
To be added after selecting a core HAI trust paper.
Research Question
How does the paper define, measure, or manipulate trust in AI?
Why It Matters
Trust can shape whether users verify AI output or accept it without scrutiny.
Study Design / Method
To be evaluated based on the selected paper’s experimental or observational design.
Key Findings
To be added with careful distinction between reported findings and interpretation.
What I Found Convincing
To be used for strengths in construct definition, measurement, or ecological validity.
What I Question
To be used for concerns about reliability, sample, task design, or generalizability.
Research Gap
Many trust studies separate perceived trust from repeated everyday interaction; this gap can connect to reliance over time.
My Research Question
Can repeated interaction with an AI system increase perceived trust even when its actual reliability remains unchanged?
How I Would Extend the Study
I would compare trust calibration across repeated interactions while holding system reliability constant.
Proposed Method
A longitudinal or repeated-task experiment measuring trust, verification behavior, confidence, and reliance after each interaction.
Expected Contribution
This would test a new HAI hypothesis inspired by, but not concluded from, prior Anua research on familiarity and consumer trust.

AI & Self-Disclosure

Possible question: “When are people more willing to disclose emotionally difficult experiences to AI than to another person?”

Paper Overview
To be added after selecting a paper on AI-mediated disclosure, reflection, or conversational agents.
Research Question
What makes people willing or unwilling to share emotionally sensitive information with AI?
Why It Matters
Disclosure to AI may create opportunities for reflection, but it also raises questions about trust, privacy, and emotional safety.
Study Design / Method
To be evaluated based on whether the paper uses interviews, experiments, diary methods, or conversational data.
Key Findings
To be added after paper selection.
What I Found Convincing
To be used for strong evidence about context, privacy perception, or social evaluation.
What I Question
To be used for concerns about sensitivity, participant protection, task realism, or measurement of disclosure depth.
Research Gap
The relationship between trust and willingness to disclose may differ across emotional intensity, perceived judgment, and perceived AI memory.
My Research Question
When are people more willing to disclose emotionally difficult experiences to AI than to another person?
How I Would Extend the Study
I would compare disclosure willingness across AI, anonymous human, and known-human conditions.
Proposed Method
A mixed-methods study combining scenario-based measures, optional written disclosure tasks, and follow-up interviews.
Expected Contribution
This could clarify when AI is experienced as nonjudgmental support and when trust concerns reduce disclosure.

Placeholder Note

Multilingual Human-AI Interaction

Possible question: “Does interacting with AI in one’s native versus second language affect trust and willingness to self-disclose?”

Paper Overview
To be added after selecting a paper on multilingual interaction, language preference, or cross-cultural HAI.
Research Question
How does language context influence trust, interpretation, emotional expression, or disclosure in AI interaction?
Why It Matters
Human-AI trust may not transfer cleanly across languages because language affects nuance, comfort, identity, and perceived accuracy.
Study Design / Method
To be evaluated based on comparison of language conditions, participant background, and task type.
Key Findings
To be added after paper selection.
What I Found Convincing
To be used for strong operationalization of language context and user experience.
What I Question
To be used for concerns about translation quality, cultural assumptions, or uneven language proficiency.
Research Gap
Multilingual context may be an important application setting for trust and self-disclosure research, not a separate research track.
My Research Question
Does interacting with AI in one’s native versus second language affect trust and willingness to self-disclose?
How I Would Extend the Study
I would compare native-language and second-language AI interaction in emotionally sensitive and informational tasks.
Proposed Method
A within-subjects mixed-methods design measuring perceived trust, comfort, verification intent, disclosure willingness, and qualitative reflections.
Expected Contribution
This could show how language context shapes the trust-to-disclosure pathway in Human-AI Interaction.
