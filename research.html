Research

Empirical and structured research projects

These projects involve research design, data collection, analysis, and methodological reflection. They are presented as foundations for my developing research agenda in Human-AI Interaction, trust, reliance, judgment, and behavior.

Project 1

Anua — Consumer Trust & Decision-Making

A consumer behavior case study examining how repeated exposure and familiarity may shape perceived trust and purchase-related behavior.

260 survey respondents 41 screened candidates 5 pilot interviews 3 in-depth interviews

Research Question

How do exposure, familiarity, and perceived trust influence consumer decision-making around a skincare brand?

Context

The project investigated consumer perceptions of Anua through survey screening and interviews, reframing campaign-oriented insights as behavioral research about trust formation.

Study Design

The study combined broad survey responses with screened qualitative follow-up to identify patterns in familiarity, perceived credibility, and decision-making.

Methods

Survey research, respondent screening, pilot interviews, in-depth interviews, and thematic analysis of consumer decision narratives.

Participant Selection

Participants were screened from a larger survey pool to identify candidates with relevant brand awareness, purchase consideration, or prior exposure.

Analysis

Responses were reviewed for recurring links between repeated exposure, familiarity, trust perception, and behavior. Interview data was used to interpret the mechanisms behind survey patterns.

Key Findings

The central observation was: repeated exposure may support familiarity, familiarity may increase perceived trust, and perceived trust may affect behavior.

Limitations

The findings are specific to a consumer-brand context and should not be treated as direct evidence about AI trust or Human-AI Interaction.

Research Reflection

This project helped me see trust as a behavioral process shaped by repeated contact, interpretation, and context rather than a fixed attitude.

How This Shaped My Research Interest

The Anua study led to a new question: might familiarity similarly influence perceived trust in AI systems? This is a new HAI hypothesis, not a conclusion from the consumer study.

Repeated Exposure → Familiarity → Perceived Trust → Behavior

Project 2

MealCare — Mixed-Methods Field Research

A methodology-centered applied research project using multiple forms of data collection to understand behavior in context.

Research Question

How can behavioral and contextual research inform practical interventions around food waste and meal-related decision-making?

Study Design

The project used a mixed-methods structure to triangulate self-reported attitudes, observed behavior, and material evidence.

Research Ethics

Research activities were designed with attention to informed participation, respectful data collection, privacy, and sensitivity around everyday habits.

Methods

Survey, interviews, focus groups, field observation, waste audit, and ethics-centered research planning.

Data Collection

Data was gathered across participant responses, group discussion, direct observation, and waste-audit evidence to compare what people said with what happened in practice.

Analysis

The analysis emphasized convergence and tension across methods, identifying where attitudes, constraints, routines, and environmental conditions shaped behavior.

Findings

The project showed the value of mixed methods for understanding applied problems that cannot be explained by survey responses alone.

Limitations

Because the work was context-specific, broader claims would require larger samples, repeated data collection, and more controlled comparison across settings.

Reflection

MealCare strengthened my interest in research designs that combine quantitative structure with qualitative interpretation and field-based observation.

Project 3

ENV338 — Quantitative Field Research

A quantitative field research project focused on hypothesis formation, sampling, measurement, and descriptive statistical comparison.

Research Question

How do measurable environmental conditions vary across selected field sites or observation points?

Hypothesis

The project began with a testable expectation about measurable differences across sites, conditions, or repeated observations.

Sampling Design

Sampling decisions considered location, timing, repeated measurement, and consistency across field observations.

Measurement

Repeated measurements were collected using a structured field protocol to support comparison and reduce avoidable inconsistency.

Analysis

Analysis emphasized mean, median, and standard deviation comparisons to describe central tendency and variation across observations.

Results

The results were interpreted through descriptive statistical patterns, with attention to what the data could and could not support.

Limitations

Limitations included sample size, environmental variability, possible measurement error, and constraints on causal interpretation.

Future Research

A stronger future design would increase repeated measurement, clarify control conditions, and strengthen the connection between hypothesis, sampling, and analysis.
